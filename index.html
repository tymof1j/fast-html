Project plan / 1

Project overview

Company: SICH, Ukraine. Project: K9 Tourniquet Development and Global Market Launch.

Team: Denys Shcherbak, Oleksii Shevchuk, Ihor Bahrii. Project managers named in the charter: Denys Shcherbak and Oleksii Shevchuk. Sponsor: SICH C-suites; owner: SICH K-9 / COO, as described in the charter.

Proposed baseline: 8 September 2026–8 May 2028, approximately 20 months. This sits within the charter’s 18–24-month estimate. The calendar is a planning proposal, not a record of completed work.

The project adapts SICH’s existing manufacturing capability to a K9 tourniquet, validates prototypes and a 500-unit pilot, launches in Ukraine and expands internationally. Closure depends on sales and campaign results, not simply the passage of time.

Project plan / 3

Scheduling approach and assumptions

Hybrid approach: predictive dependencies and formal stage gates for investment, design, manufacturing, testing and launch; adaptive design iterations after prototype and field feedback, plus campaign adjustments during sales monitoring. Weekly team reviews track blockers; monthly sponsor reviews assess funding, gate readiness and forecast dates.

All dates are inclusive and durations are elapsed calendar days, not person-days or working days. Work packages span weekends and holidays; staffing and working calendars must be confirmed during feasibility. No separate contingency reserve is quantified. A failed safety or commercial gate triggers corrective work and a revised forecast.

One accountable role is listed per activity; role allocations are proposed, using the charter’s specialist roles. Departmental support and specialist availability are assumed. “Project Manager” means one designated lead from the two named managers, to be assigned internally.

The 26 original work packages remain intact. Schedule suffixes a/b divide 1.2.2 into initial and final design, 1.2.3 into patent search and filing, and 1.6.2 into submission and review/readiness: 29 scheduled activities. These are scheduling details under existing packages, not new Level-2 deliverables.

Prototype safety review precedes handler testing. Initial prototype sign-off does not authorize commercial launch. Supplier contracts in 1.4.1 must be reconciled with final specifications before production. Activity 1.4.3 includes lab clearance before field work; 1.4.4 includes corrective verification and retesting; 1.4.5 includes the pilot supply-chain and safety/quality decision. Scale-up verification follows in 1.5.2.

Project plan / 8

Sales measurement and closure

Ukraine: weighted monthly units = non-grant-funded units + 0.5 × grant-funded units. Classify units once; do not count grant-funded units again as ordinary sales. Target: at least 5,000 weighted units per interval. Example: 4,000 ordinary units + 2,000 grant-funded units × 0.5 = 5,000 weighted units.

International: track non-Ukrainian sales separately. Proposed measurement periods are 8 February–7 March, 8 March–7 April, and 8 April–7 May 2028. The source wording is an average of at least USD 400,000 per month across three consecutive months: the three-month total must be at least USD 1.2 million. Show each month’s result as well as the average. This does not silently replace the source with a stricter “each month must exceed USD 400,000” rule.

Finance must agree the revenue basis, treatment of returns and currency conversion before measurement. International campaign KPI values are not supplied in the documents: define and approve them in 1.2.4, then report against them in 1.6.3 and at closure. Sales targets are acceptance conditions, not guaranteed outcomes. If they are not achieved, extend monitoring and revise the closure forecast.

The charter’s longer-term ambition of manufacturing capacity worth up to USD 2 million is a strategic aspiration, not an additional quantified closure requirement in this schedule.

Project plan / 9

Schedule analysis for the group presentation

Main sequence: feasibility → requirements and initial design → prototype validation → final design/IP filing → 500-unit manufacture → lab/field testing → fixes and Go/No-Go → scale-up and domestic launch → international readiness and distribution → three-month sales evidence → closure.

Parallel work: finance and funding identification proceed together; anatomy, patent search and requirements mapping run in parallel after business-case approval. Supplier work overlaps prototype validation. Regulatory research starts early. Manufacturing expansion, supply-chain verification and domestic launch planning run in parallel after Go/No-Go.

Schedule pressure: the pilot and acceptance gates leave limited recovery time before the October launch-readiness target. The international access review and February distribution preparation are ambitious assumptions. Delays in either move the sales observation window and the May closure forecast. This is a dependency-based assessment, not a resource-loaded critical-path calculation.

Risks and responses: secure lab, veterinary and K9-unit availability early; keep safety and retesting gates mandatory; validate supplier capacity and alternatives; confirm funding before committing downstream resources; revisit design when market-access research identifies requirements; check weighted demand so grants do not overstate sustainability; review logistics and currency exposure through Finance and Procurement.

Change control: retain baseline and forecast dates separately, record actual starts/finishes, and escalate missed gates with an impact assessment. Source governance assigns material scope, budget, safety and stage-gate changes to the sponsor and owner.
